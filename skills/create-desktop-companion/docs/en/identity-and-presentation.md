# Identity-safe character presentation

[English](../en/identity-and-presentation.md) · [简体中文](../zh-CN/identity-and-presentation.md)

## 1. Approve layout and artwork separately

An accepted composition does not approve distorted characters drawn inside it. Use a concept board only for section placement, hierarchy, typography and spacing. Restore the latest applicable approved character source instead of regenerating the whole board as one new illustration. Keep layout configuration and character art in separate files.

The combined presentation contains genuine action loops, one character demonstrating the required attention directions, and the requested static body views. The current editorial example uses large gaze on the left, four views in one row below it, and nine action panels on the right. This is a configurable example, not a permanent aspect ratio or host requirement. Confirm the latest reference's actual pixels and distinguish layout approval from character, pose, material and final acceptance.

Align viewports, gutters, outer edges and text baselines. Do not force unlike silhouettes to match, independently fit-scale each pose, stretch anatomy or push airborne frames back onto the ground. Short labels are bilingual: specific action first, separate state/purpose second. Keep evidence and limitations in companion notes, not tiny footer paragraphs.

## 2. Establish identity and actual provenance

Record the applicable approved reference and its version/hash. Lock body proportions, head/body relationship, limb count and attachment, limb length, markings, tail count/root/relative length/shape, and asymmetric accessories. Tail movement may be natural; a different tail, drifting root or extra limb is not.

Identity locking is not pixel freezing. Plausible perspective, articulation, head-following accessories and natural occlusion are allowed. Check attachment, count, relative size and consistent reappearance. Unseen rear patterns remain disclosed design inference, not photographic or canonical proof. A rejected concept cannot replace the approved identity reference.

Keep old installed art, newly generated source frames, concept layouts and final composed exports in separate evidence chains. Record actual input versions/hashes, inspected-file hash, QA scope, and verified relationships. Unknown relationships remain unknown: an old asset's defect does not prove a newer asset defective; earlier source approval does not approve a generated concept or encoded export. Dates, names and resemblance are not lineage proof.

## 3. Gaze is attention, not a body turntable

Use the adapter's verified order. In the dated Pets v2 example, 000 is up, 090 screen-right, 180 down and 270 screen-left; sixteen samples advance clockwise by 22.5 degrees. They are not sixteen task states, a neutral-plus-fifteen set or sixteen full-body rotations.

Maintain comparable body placement and scale. Eyes and limited head/neck movement are usual cues, adapted to the actual anatomy. Attached ears, hair, hats and glasses may move with the head; their identity and attachment must remain consistent. Do not use stretching torsos, newly shaped tails or swapped bodies to show direction. Faceless or mechanical characters must use existing, explicitly described directional cues; do not invent eyes, a neck or independent components. Disclose unreadable directions instead of claiming perfect gaze.

Check all sixteen actual images at intended display size and with anatomical close-ups. Inspect cardinal meaning, neighboring continuity, opposite-direction distinction, tail/root/limbs and asymmetric details. Sixteen occupied or different cells prove neither direction nor identity. A sweep preview proves ordered playback only, not native pointer tracking or interpolation. Use the target's required production and repair pipeline; this method never waives a mandatory gate.

## 4. Design each character, not one generic gesture set

Use observed individual/species behavior, verified same-version IP performances, or anatomy-compatible conventional gestures. Identify observations, adaptations and fallback choices explicitly. A natural action does not inherently mean a software status. Preserve good approved actions; mark uninspected revisions unreviewed rather than redesigning them from an older atlas.

For every track, record one selected action, attention target, source, concise rationale, nearest confusing action and visible distinction. Include one or two character-specific details that improve readability, not arbitrary props. Separate the four business meanings from nine animation tracks and sixteen attention samples. Sustained processing stays processing for the entire loop; a request remains directed to the user; Ready means output is already available to inspect, not endless unfinished work. Completion gestures are not universally prohibited: judge the actual full-cycle meaning. Idle, locomotion, greeting and jump are not forced into the four business labels.

Preserve explicitly approved transformations. Verify their visible progress, destination form and identity continuity in the actual frames, not merely a later static pose. A processing pose after a transformation cannot substitute for the transformation itself. Runtime triggering remains independently verified or unknown.

## 5. Inspect source and final output separately

Inspect every delivered frame for identity, anatomy, meaning, direction, timing and crop. Compare the last two and first two poses; check support, takeoff/landing, scale, texture, appendage follow-through and meaningful holds. Review the whole loop unlabeled against its confusing neighbors. A recognized first pose does not establish sustained meaning.

After composition, resampling, palette conversion or encoding, decode and review the actual final GIF/video again. Record what was generated, decoded, visually inspected and actually watched separately. Geometry checks, hashes and encoder exit codes do not establish likeness, comfort or runtime behavior. If encoded bytes change after upload, compare decoded RGBA where appropriate; equivalence proves content preservation only. Keep native runtime, pointer triggers and long timed viewing unverified until actually observed.

## 6. Representative covers, iteration and delivery

Choose the most informative reviewed source frame per track for the static overview. Frame zero is allowed when genuinely representative, not used automatically. Record source version/hash, index and rationale. Different panels may show different phases: label this a representative-pose overview, not one simultaneous animation timestamp. Do not reorder or retime animation to make the cover look better.

For each iteration record observed defect, authorized scope, actual changes, inspected artifact, results and remaining limits. Preserve earlier versions, failed samples and actual executed prompts; proposed prompts are not generation evidence.

Deliver the combined animation, representative static overview, private source/reproduction package and concise per-character design/iteration note. The method package must remain separate from private art, photos and identifiers. Production authorization is not installation, activation, public redistribution or repository publication permission. Method updates alone do not fix or certify character art.


## Cross-row scale registration before mixing sources

A newly extracted strip passing its own row checks does not mean it can be mixed with an older atlas at the same nominal multiplier. Extraction may have used a different scale or anchor. Review state changes between the approved idle/reference row and every new row before accepting the combined atlas or showcase.

Use an approved neutral or comparable pose as the identity anchor. Compare stable head and torso feature dimensions, body proportions and the intended foot/contact or hover anchor at a shared physical scale. A frame's overall bounding-box height may grow because a paw, wing or tail extends or a jump leaves the ground; it is not a reliable standalone measure of identity scale. If no comparable pose exists, record that uncertainty and use multiple stable landmarks rather than forcing equal height.

Preserve the original extracted frames and their extraction scale/translation. When a source group is consistently oversized or undersized, apply one documented shared scale and registration transform to that complete group, aligned to the reference contact anchor. Keep the same transform across its frames and any rows that share that source registration. Do not fit each frame to its own bounding box, normalize all poses to identical height, or erase airborne displacement. If the mismatch is inconsistent within a group, correct the extraction or source artwork rather than concealing it with per-frame fitting.

Record source version/hash, original extraction transform, reference neutral frame and landmarks, measured feature sizes with units, selected group scale/translation, anchor, affected frames, and final registered source hashes. Recheck idle → each changed row → idle, identity at normal display size, contact/airborne motion, and loop seams. Separately inspect the actual encoded output after composition. A numeric scale correction is evidence of registration, not an automatic verdict on anatomy, likeness or semantic quality.
